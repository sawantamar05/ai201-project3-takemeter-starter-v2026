# Milestone 2: taxonomy boundary check

**Status:** AI-assisted draft for student review. No dataset annotation,
acceptance-criteria writing, or training was performed in this milestone.

The proposed labels, one-sentence definitions, six real examples, and ordered
decision rules are in the README's **Label Taxonomy** section. The same
definitions are in `label_definitions.txt`, and the notebook label map is
`analysis`, `request`, `commentary` in that order.

## Why these three labels

The community reading showed detailed questions and feature requests alongside
supported evaluations, unsupported opinions, and reactions. The starter's
example labels `analysis`, `hot_take`, and `reaction` do not clearly accommodate
those requests. `request` gives them a place, while `commentary` covers the
remaining reactions, unsupported opinions, and simple resource shares.

Personal experience is a possible source of support within `analysis`, rather
than a separate label that would overlap with analytical comments. Positive
and negative sentiment can occur in every label.

## Real borderline comment: two defensible readings

Source: [gitowiec's notation complaint](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50132341).
The complete comment quotes a mathematical passage, describes trying to change
browser settings, and says the writer feels excluded by the notation.

**Case for `analysis`:** The complaint supplies an identifiable example of the
notation and a reported troubleshooting outcome. These details explain what
caused the accessibility problem rather than merely announcing dislike.

**Case for `commentary`:** The emotional appraisal is prominent, and much of
the apparent technical substance belongs to a quoted passage. Quoting material
is not automatically the same as independently analyzing it.

The decision rule resolves the case by requiring a main claim and relevant
support in the comment. The proposed result is `analysis` because the writer
uses the quoted example and attempted fix to support the accessibility complaint.
Its correctness is not independently verified; this taxonomy measures the
support offered in the text. The student should review and take ownership of
the rule before applying it to the dataset.

## Eight synthetic boundary posts

These posts were generated by AI solely to challenge the definitions. They
are not real Hacker News comments and must not enter `labels.csv` or be marked
`cold`. The following choices and rationales are an AI-assisted application of
the proposed rules.

### 1. A measurement followed by a genuine request

> The model answered eight of my ten SQL questions correctly, but both failures involved joins. Would adding schema examples help, or should I change models?

**Proposed label:** `request`.

**Reason:** The measured result sets up an unresolved request for advice. The
author does not answer whether changing the prompt or model will help.

### 2. A rhetorical question with a concrete comparison

> Who would call this release reliable? In the same ten-task test, it crashed three times while the previous version completed every task.

**Proposed label:** `analysis`.

**Reason:** The question expresses a reliability judgment. The same-task
comparison supplies support for that judgment; no answer from readers is needed.

### 3. Experience without a described result

> I've used this model for a month. It feels much smarter than everything else, and I'm definitely renewing.

**Proposed label:** `commentary`.

**Reason:** Duration of use is specific, but does not substantiate the claim
that the model is smarter. No task outcome, comparison, or mechanism is described.

### 4. A bare resource share

> The author posted a shorter explanation here: https://example.org/summary. Sharing it for anyone who found the original difficult.

**Proposed label:** `commentary`.

**Reason:** The link shares a resource without developing an assessment or
asking for an answer or action. A link alone is not qualifying support.

### 5. An explanatory mechanism without a benchmark

> Long prompts slow generation because the model must access a larger attention cache for each new token. A short-prompt speed test therefore misses that cost.

**Proposed label:** `analysis`.

**Reason:** The mechanism connects prompt length to a benchmark limitation.
An explicit explanation can qualify without a number or external link; this
exercise does not establish that the technical explanation is correct.

### 6. A quoted-wording reaction

> The announcement calls this a tiny update and then lists major improvements in every category. That wording made me laugh.

**Proposed label:** `commentary`.

**Reason:** The wording is the object of a humorous reaction. The writer does
not state an additional claim about a result, behavior, limitation, accessibility
problem, or implication. The clarified rule forbids inferring an analytical
conclusion solely from the juxtaposed wording.

### 7. A detailed hypothetical question

> Suppose I store fifty state-capital pairs as vectors, then test the reverse mapping. Would successful reverse lookup show generalization, or only memorization?

**Proposed label:** `request`.

**Reason:** The concrete example poses an unresolved question. Its detail does
not make it a supported answer or conclusion.

### 8. An explicit feature request

> Please add multi-GPU support, a PCIe-generation selector, and an option for splitting layers between CPU and GPU. Those are the configurations I want to compare.

**Proposed label:** `request`.

**Reason:** This directly asks for concrete changes, so the label includes
requests for action as well as questions seeking information.

## What the stress test changed

- Replaced the narrower candidate name `opinion` with `commentary`, explicitly
  covering resource shares as well as opinions and reactions.
- Used `request` instead of `question` to cover explicit feature requests
  observed in the reading sample.
- Defined a primary request before checking for support: technical background
  and hypothetical examples can set up a request without answering it.
- Distinguished genuine questions from rhetorical questions or questions
  emphasizing an already-demonstrated inconsistency.
- Required support to bear on the writer's main claim; a number, model name,
  quotation, or link by itself does not determine the label.
- An independent AI application initially found post 6 ambiguous: the wording
  contrast might imply an analytical criticism. The rule was tightened to
  require an expressed substantive claim, rather than an inferred conclusion
  behind a joke or feeling. The explicit final choice is `commentary`.
- Included the essential primary-request precedence and reaction restriction
  in the matching one-sentence definitions themselves, so the next unit's
  baseline receives them even though it does not read the README's longer rules.

The eight proposed choices exercise each label and the mixed-purpose boundaries.
This is a clarity check, not a measurement of human agreement or model accuracy.
Any student revisions should be made consistently in README.md and
`label_definitions.txt`, with label-name changes also reflected in the notebook.
